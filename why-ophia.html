Why Ophia

Where eye-care software usually falls short, and what Ophia does instead.

Ophthalmologists' complaints about their record systems are well documented: slow data entry, poor drawing tools, systems that don't talk to each other, and surgery kept on paper. Ophia was built at a working eye hospital with those problems in front of it.

AreaCommon in eye-care softwareIn Ophia

Scope

Clinic records that stop at the consultation room, with surgery, the ward and patients' access handled elsewhere or on paper.

One record from booking to discharge: front desk, optometry, consultation, counselling, ward, theatre and the patient app.

Data entry

Generic forms with many clicks per visit, and the same details typed again at each desk.

Each desk's screen fits its job. History, allergies, the eye and the procedure carry forward to the next desk and stay editable.

Two eyes

One field where an eye clinic needs a right and a left, so findings end up in free text.

Right and left side by side for vision, refraction, eye pressure, findings and diagnosis, with one click to mark an eye normal or copy it across.

Refraction

Readings taken on paper or another system, then copied in by the doctor.

A refraction desk on the same record, with its own queue. The doctor opens the visit with the readings already there.

Drawings

Drawing tools that are slow and coarse, so many doctors stop using them.

Drawings on the hospital's own charts of the anterior segment, cornea, lids, gonioscopy and fundus, kept with the visit.

Surgery

Pre-operative, operative and post-operative notes kept apart, and safety checklists on paper.

One linked surgical path from counselling and consent to the ward checklist, theatre checklists, implants, operative record and discharge. Theatre won't start a case without signed consent.

Prescriptions

Each doctor writes a separate slip, and tapering eye-drop courses are left to the patient to work out.

One prescription per patient per day, even when several doctors see them, and tapers that always fit inside the course.

Old paper records

A scanner at one desk, or files that never make it into the system.

Any phone can add them. Staff scan a QR code and photograph the pages, and they land in that patient's file.

Patients

No patient app, or a generic one with no link to the clinical record.

A patient app on the same record, with booking and payment, the live queue, prescriptions, reports and family access.

Hosting and lock-in

Cloud-only or install-only, with data in a format only the vendor can read.

On your premises or in the cloud, the same software. Data lives in a standard PostgreSQL database the hospital can export.

Common complaints are drawn from the American Academy of Ophthalmology's survey of members' EHRs (EyeNet), a review of ophthalmology's requirements for record systems (PMC), and buyer reviews of ophthalmology software (Software Advice).
